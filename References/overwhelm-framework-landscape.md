# The Overwhelm Framework Landscape

A working map of the somatic, contemplative, and therapeutic frameworks adjacent to Whelm’s three-move arc (Signal → Tangle → Portal) and its brain-dump → sift → deepen → map method. Compiled so Lorin can position, cite, and differentiate without overclaiming or quietly reinventing something that already exists.

## 1. How to read this doc

Each framework below gets a one-line mechanism, originator, publication status, and 2–5 verbatim quotes with inline source URLs. Quotes are exact; every factual claim carries a link so it can be fact-checked. Section 3 maps Whelm’s moves against each framework — where they overlap, where they’re silent, where Whelm potentially adds. Section 4 is the honest read on novelty. Section 5 offers positioning options. Section 6 is a citation-ready shortlist. Section 7 catalogs gaps and ideological loads.

The strongest empirical anchors here are MBSR, ACT, and (with caveats) Polyvagal Theory and Self-Compassion. Most somatic and contemplative frameworks below are practitioner-developed, popularized through trade books and trainings, and have light-to-moderate peer-reviewed support. That is true of FACE, RAIN, IFS, SE, Hakomi, and Focusing alike. None of this disqualifies them as references; it just means Whelm should cite them as practitioner lineage, not as evidence base.

## 2. Framework-by-framework

### 2.1 RAIN — Tara Brach (popularizer) / Michele McDonald (originator)

- **Originator of acronym:** Michele McDonald, Vipassana teacher, ~2000s.
- **Popularizer:** Tara Brach, PhD, clinical psychologist and meditation teacher.
- **Mechanism:** A four-step mindfulness sequence for working with difficult emotion: Recognize, Allow, Investigate, Nurture.
- **Publication status:** Practitioner framework. Brach’s book *Radical Compassion* (Viking, 2019) is the main long-form treatment. Peer-reviewed evidence specific to RAIN as a named protocol is limited; the underlying mechanisms (mindfulness + self-compassion) are heavily studied via MBSR and MSC.

Verbatim:

- “Recognize what is happening / Allow the experience to be there, just as it is / Investigate with interest and care / Nurture with self-compassion” ([tarabrach.com/rain](https://www.tarabrach.com/rain/)).
- (On origin) “insight meditation teacher Michele McDonald introduced the RAIN practice about 20 years ago … McDonald, who is cofounder of Vipassana Hawaii, coined the acronym RAIN for Recognition of what is going on; Acceptance of the experience, just as it is; Interest in what is happening; and Non-Identification to depersonalize the experience” ([mindful.org](https://www.mindful.org/investigate-anxiety-with-tara-brachs-rain-practice/)).
- (On Brach’s modification) “over the years, Tara Brach modified and popularized RAIN, shifting the ‘N’ step to Nurture and suggesting non-identification as a product of her revised four steps” ([mindful.org](https://www.mindful.org/investigate-anxiety-with-tara-brachs-rain-practice/)).
- (Investigate step) “Instead of thinking about what’s going on, keep bringing your attention to your body” — Tara Brach ([mindful.org](https://www.mindful.org/investigate-anxiety-with-tara-brachs-rain-practice/)).
- (Nurture step) “Call upon the most wise and compassionate part of your being” — Tara Brach ([mindful.org](https://www.mindful.org/investigate-anxiety-with-tara-brachs-rain-practice/)).

**Research caution:** No peer-reviewed RCT located that tests RAIN as a discrete protocol. Adjacent self-compassion research (Neff & Germer, MSC) shows medium-to-large effect sizes for self-compassion interventions ([Neff 2023, Annual Review of Psychology](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf)), which generalizes plausibly to RAIN since its “N” step is explicitly a self-compassion move.

### 2.2 FACE — Catherine Liggett

- **Originator:** Catherine Liggett, holistic mental health coach and shadow-work teacher.
- **Mechanism:** A four-step somatic sequence for emotional regulation in the moment: Feel, Amplify, Connect, Embrace.
- **Publication status:** Practitioner framework. No peer-reviewed support located. The corpus is small: Liggett’s own site, Insight Timer profile, and a single guest post on the Generation Mindful blog where the acronym is laid out in full. Liggett does not, in any source located, name RAIN or Tara Brach as inspiration for FACE.

Verbatim (all from Liggett’s Generation Mindful guest post):

- “I call this process **FACE**: Feel, Amplify, Connect, and Embrace” ([genmindful.com](https://genmindful.com/blogs/mindful-moments/how-to-become-a-cycle-breaking-parent-in-4-steps)).
- (Feel) “In this step, I notice the body sensations that are moving through me, and name the feeling” ([genmindful.com](https://genmindful.com/blogs/mindful-moments/how-to-become-a-cycle-breaking-parent-in-4-steps)).
- (Amplify) “Breathing and allowing this feeling to be in my body, I now take deepening breaths as I imagine opening space around it, and inviting it to take up as much space as it needs in the room around me” ([genmindful.com](https://genmindful.com/blogs/mindful-moments/how-to-become-a-cycle-breaking-parent-in-4-steps)).
- (Connect) The step prompts “When did I feel this as a child?” to uncover the developmental root of the reactivity ([genmindful.com](https://genmindful.com/blogs/mindful-moments/how-to-become-a-cycle-breaking-parent-in-4-steps)).
- (Embrace) “Imagine now that you are looking at the child version of yourself through the eyes of the strongest and most loving version of your adult self” ([genmindful.com](https://genmindful.com/blogs/mindful-moments/how-to-become-a-cycle-breaking-parent-in-4-steps)).

**Relationship to RAIN:** Structurally parallel. Both are four-step protocols that move from recognition → allowing → investigation → compassion. FACE’s **Amplify** is the move that distinguishes it: rather than only allowing, it actively *grows* the feeling within tolerance — which is the element Lorin has named as influencing Whelm. FACE’s **Connect** is closer to an IFS / inner-child move than RAIN’s “Investigate.” Liggett does not publicly credit RAIN, so any claim that FACE is RAIN-derived is Lorin’s inference, not Liggett’s stated lineage. Use “structurally parallel to RAIN” rather than “derived from RAIN” unless Liggett confirms.

**Caution:** The public corpus on FACE is one blog post and Liggett’s site copy. If Whelm leans on FACE as a primary reference, Lorin should consider asking Liggett directly for attribution language. (See positioning option b in §5.)

### 2.3 Focusing — Eugene Gendlin

- **Originator:** Eugene T. Gendlin, philosopher and psychotherapist, University of Chicago.
- **Mechanism:** A six-step body-centered process for letting an unclear bodily knowing (the “felt sense”) reveal a word, image, or phrase that names what is actually going on — and then deepening on that handle.
- **Publication status:** Book — *Focusing*, Bantam, 1978 (2nd ed. 1982) ([Wikipedia: Focusing (psychotherapy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focusing_(psychotherapy))). Gendlin’s prior empirical work at U Chicago on what distinguished successful psychotherapy clients ([Wikipedia: Eugene Gendlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Gendlin)) is the research backbone. Focusing-Oriented Therapy has a moderate peer-reviewed evidence base.

Verbatim:

- (Felt sense definition) “the unclear, pre-verbal sense of ‘something’ — the inner knowledge or awareness that has not been consciously thought or verbalized — as that ‘something’ is experienced in the body” ([Wikipedia: Focusing (psychotherapy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focusing_(psychotherapy))).
- (The six steps, summarized on the International Focusing Institute’s page) “1. Clearing a Space … 2. Felt Sense … 3. Get a Handle … 4. Resonating … 5. Asking … 6. Receiving” ([focusing.org/sixsteps](https://focusing.org/sixsteps)).
- (Step 3 — “Get a Handle”) “What is the quality of the felt sense? What one word, phrase, or image comes out of this felt sense?” ([focusing.org/sixsteps](https://focusing.org/sixsteps)).
- (Step 4 — “Resonating”) “Go back and forth between the felt sense and the word, phrase, or image and feel how they resonate with each other. See if there is a little bodily signal that lets you know there is a fit” ([focusing.org/sixsteps](https://focusing.org/sixsteps)).
- (On not adhering rigidly) “the essence of Focusing is not adhering to these steps, but following the organic process” ([Wikipedia: Focusing (psychotherapy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focusing_(psychotherapy))).

**This is the closest existing parallel to Whelm’s “sift for the potent word/phrase” move.** Gendlin called the same gesture “getting a handle.” Lorin should know this exists; she is doing a recognizable thing in a recognized lineage.

### 2.4 Window of Tolerance — Dan Siegel

- **Originator:** Daniel J. Siegel, MD, clinical professor of psychiatry, UCLA.
- **Mechanism:** A model of an “optimal zone of arousal” bounded above by hyperarousal (panic, flooding) and below by hypoarousal (numbing, dissociation). Healing and learning happen inside the window; outside it, the nervous system protects rather than integrates.
- **Publication status:** Book — *The Developing Mind: Toward a Neurobiology of Interpersonal Experience*, Guilford Press, 1999. Concept is widely adopted in trauma therapy and has substantial practitioner uptake; it is not itself a peer-reviewed construct with a discrete measurement instrument, though related constructs (arousal regulation, autonomic flexibility) are.

Verbatim:

- (Definition) “the optimal zone of arousal in which an individual can function effectively — emotionally regulated, cognitively flexible, and socially engaged” ([Psychology Tools — Window of Tolerance](https://www.psychologytools.com/resource/window-of-tolerance)).
- (Hyperarousal) “When arousal exceeds the upper threshold, clients may experience hyperarousal (e.g., panic, irritability, impulsivity)” ([iptrauma.org](https://iptrauma.org/docs/body-of-knowledge-of-psychotraumatology/understanding-the-window-of-tolerance-in-trauma-theory/)).
- (Hypoarousal) “dropping below the lower threshold can lead to hypoarousal (e.g., emotional numbing, dissociation, withdrawal)” ([iptrauma.org](https://iptrauma.org/docs/body-of-knowledge-of-psychotraumatology/understanding-the-window-of-tolerance-in-trauma-theory/)).

**This is the frame Liggett’s “Amplify” borrows from, and the frame Whelm borrows in turn.** Amplifying a feeling only helps if it stays inside the window; outside the window, amplification becomes flooding.

### 2.5 Somatic Experiencing — Peter Levine

- **Originator:** Peter A. Levine, PhD, medical biophysics and psychology.
- **Mechanism:** A body-based trauma renegotiation method that uses *titration* (working with small doses of activation) and *pendulation* (oscillating between expansion and contraction) to discharge survival energy without re-traumatization.
- **Publication status:** Books — *Waking the Tiger* (North Atlantic, 1997), *In an Unspoken Voice* (2010). Practitioner-developed; peer-reviewed evidence base is growing but moderate (most rigorous trials are small or pilot-stage).

Verbatim:

- (Titration) “Titration is the slow release of compressed survival energy … because trauma is ‘too much, too fast, too soon,’ we want to counter this in trauma renegotiation … the remembering happens in droplets” ([sarahrossphd.com](https://sarahrossphd.com/resourcing-pendulation-titration-practices-somatic-experiencing/)).
- (Pendulation) “Pendulation is a term coined by Dr. Levine to describe the natural oscillation between opposing forces of contraction and expansion … This ebb and flow allows the polarities to gradually be integrated” ([sarahrossphd.com](https://sarahrossphd.com/resourcing-pendulation-titration-practices-somatic-experiencing/)).
- (Levine, on freezing) “Animals do not view freezing as a sign of inadequacy or weakness, nor should we” ([somaticexperiencing.com](https://www.somaticexperiencing.com/somatic-experiencing)).
- (Levine, on the body) “The body has been designed to renew itself through continuous self-correction. These same principles also apply to the healing of psyche, spirit, and soul” ([somaticexperiencing.com](https://www.somaticexperiencing.com/somatic-experiencing)).

**Relevance to Whelm:** Titration is the discipline behind Liggett’s “amplify within window of tolerance.” If Whelm names overwhelm as a heightened state with potential value (the Portal move), Levine is the closest existing legitimization of that move at the nervous-system level.

### 2.6 Internal Family Systems (IFS) — Richard Schwartz

- **Originator:** Richard C. Schwartz, PhD, family therapist.
- **Mechanism:** A non-pathologizing model that treats the psyche as composed of “parts” (subpersonalities) led by a core “Self.” Therapy is unburdening parts so Self can lead.
- **Publication status:** Book — *Internal Family Systems Therapy* (Guilford, 1995). Has growing peer-reviewed support; IFS was listed by SAMHSA’s National Registry of Evidence-Based Programs and Practices for general functioning and well-being (the registry was retired in 2018; the listing is widely cited).

Verbatim:

- (Parts) “parts are forced into extreme roles by external circumstances and, once it seems safe, they gladly transform into valuable family members” — Richard Schwartz ([IFS Institute](https://ifs-institute.com/resources/articles/evolution-internal-family-systems-model-dr-richard-schwartz-ph-d)).
- (Self) “everyone is at their core a Self containing many crucial leadership qualities such as perspective, confidence, compassion, and acceptance” — Richard Schwartz ([IFS Institute](https://ifs-institute.com/resources/articles/evolution-internal-family-systems-model-dr-richard-schwartz-ph-d)).
- (Self-energy) “Self-led people were able to hold their center, knowing that it was just a part of them that was upset now and would eventually calm down” — Richard Schwartz ([IFS Institute](https://ifs-institute.com/resources/articles/evolution-internal-family-systems-model-dr-richard-schwartz-ph-d)).
- (The 8 Cs of Self) “calmness, curiosity, clarity, compassion, confidence, creativity, courage, and connectedness” ([Wikipedia: Internal Family Systems Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Family_Systems_Model)).

**Relevance to Whelm:** Whelm’s “Tangle” move — overwhelm as a conflict between unmet need and internalized expectation — is structurally an IFS move. The unmet-need voice and the internalized-expectation voice are, in IFS terms, two parts in conflict. IFS is the most direct existing language for what Whelm is doing in Tangle, even though Whelm does not use parts-work vocabulary.

### 2.7 Hakomi — Ron Kurtz

- **Originator:** Ron Kurtz, 1934–2011.
- **Mechanism:** A mindfulness-centered somatic psychotherapy that works *in* mindfulness (the client enters a mindful state during the session) and uses small, somatic experiments to surface implicit beliefs held in the body.
- **Publication status:** Book — *Body-Centered Psychotherapy: The Hakomi Method* (LifeRhythm, 1990). Practitioner-developed; smaller research base than SE or IFS.

Verbatim:

- (Origin and Buddhist/Taoist scaffolding) “Kurtz created a therapy that included Taoist-based principles of non-violence and supporting defenses, the Buddhist practice of mindfulness (Hakomi uniquely works with the client IN mindfulness); and unique techniques in somatic awareness” ([Hakomi Institute — Ron Kurtz](https://hakomiinstitute.com/about/ron-kurtz/)).
- (Five core principles) “Mindfulness … Nonviolence … Organicity … Unity … Body-Mind Integration” ([theembodylab.com](https://www.theembodylab.com/blog/exploring-the-hakomi-method-of-mindful-psychotherapy)).
- (Body-as-record) “the body carries memories and emotional imprints. Through mindful awareness of bodily sensations, unconscious beliefs and emotional experiences can be brought to the surface” ([theembodylab.com](https://www.theembodylab.com/blog/exploring-the-hakomi-method-of-mindful-psychotherapy)).

**Relevance to Whelm:** Hakomi’s organicity principle (“each individual has an innate ability to grow and heal at their own pace”) and its insistence that the body holds the implicit material that the conscious mind has not yet articulated is the same epistemological move Whelm’s “Portal” depends on — that the overwhelmed state itself carries information.

### 2.8 Brief mentions

**Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) — Jon Kabat-Zinn.** “Moment-to-moment, non-judgmental awareness” ([Wikipedia: MBSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindfulness-based_stress_reduction)). Developed at UMass Medical School in the late 1970s; 8-week protocol; strong peer-reviewed evidence for stress, anxiety, and depression reduction ([PMC4203918](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4203918/)). Relevant as the most empirically validated mindfulness ancestor of every framework above.

**Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) — Steven C. Hayes.** Cognitive defusion is defined as techniques that “attempt to alter the undesirable functions of thoughts and other private events, rather than trying to alter their form, frequency or situational sensitivity” ([Wikipedia: ACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acceptance_and_commitment_therapy)). The defusion move — looking *at* a thought rather than *from* it — is what Whelm’s “brain-dump and sift” does on paper: it externalizes the thought stream so it can be observed.

**Polyvagal Theory — Stephen Porges.** “In conditions of safety, ventral vagal pathways support calm states and social engagement … often described as a ‘vagal brake’” ([Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2025.1659083/full)). The physiological backbone under Window of Tolerance and SE. Note: Polyvagal Theory is widely adopted clinically but has drawn substantive scientific critique on the comparative-anatomy claims; cite it as influential, not settled.

**Self-Compassion — Kristin Neff.** Six elements: “increased self-kindness, common humanity, and mindfulness as well as reduced self-judgment, isolation, and overidentification” ([Neff 2023, Annual Review of Psychology](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf)). RCTs of Mindful Self-Compassion show medium-to-large effects on self-compassion, stress, depression, mindfulness, self-criticism, and anxiety ([Neff & Germer 2013](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/publications/Neff-Germer-MSC-RCT-2012.pdf)). Strongest empirical support for the “compassion” move that RAIN’s N and FACE’s E both perform.

**What this shows:**
- Whelm’s **method** (brain-dump → sift → deepen → map) most closely matches **Focusing**. Focusing has the sifting step and the deepening step. Whelm’s brain-dump and map are additions.
- Whelm’s **Signal move** is well-covered everywhere; that move is not novel and shouldn’t be claimed as such.
- Whelm’s **Tangle move** is most cleanly held by **IFS**. If Lorin names parts language, she’s in IFS territory. If she doesn’t, she’s doing IFS-shaped work in plainer English.
- Whelm’s **Portal move** — overwhelm as opportunity, the heightened state as informational — overlaps with Focusing, SE, IFS, and Hakomi. The phrasing “Portal” and the *commitment to that framing as a core move* (not a side note) is the most defensible piece of new language.
- The **map artifact** at the end — a tangible written record of the brain-dump → sift → deepen process that the user keeps and returns to — is the move with the least direct precedent. Focusing handles accumulate informally; Whelm proposes formalizing them.

## 4. Where Whelm is genuinely novel

Honest read: **Whelm is a recombination, not a discovery.** Every individual move has precedent. What is potentially new is:

1. **The three-move arc as a coherent semantic frame.** Signal / Tangle / Portal is a *naming* move more than a *mechanism* move. None of the frameworks above bundle exactly these three into one arc with these three names. The arc itself — body-signal → relational/internal conflict → access to inner knowing — recombines RAIN’s recognize/investigate, IFS’s parts-in-conflict, and SE/Focusing/Hakomi’s “the body holds the key.” The novelty is in the synthesis and the language, not in the underlying claims.
2. **The brain-dump → sift → deepen → map pipeline as a self-led, written artifact.** Focusing is closest, but Focusing is verbal and session-based. Whelm proposes a *written* product the user generates alone and returns to. This is plausibly new as a designed tool, though it lives in obvious adjacency to journaling, parts-mapping in IFS workbooks, and Liggett-style guided introspection.
3. **The framing of overwhelm specifically (not anxiety, not stress, not trauma) as the entry point.** Most adjacent frameworks address the broader categories of distress; Whelm picks overwhelm specifically and treats it as the diagnostic surface. Whether that framing holds up is partly an empirical question (see the Dael et al. 2025 study in the foundation doc — “overwhelm” as a *distinct affective state* is itself a recent claim).

**The most defensible claim of novelty** is the third: *Whelm picks overwhelm as a distinct state worth its own protocol, and offers a self-led written method for working with it.* Everything else is synthesis and should be presented that way.

**Claims to avoid:**
- Avoid: “Whelm is a new framework for emotional regulation.” That is overclaiming; MBSR, ACT, IFS, SE, and Hakomi are emotional regulation frameworks with decades of work behind them.
- Avoid: “Whelm’s body-as-signal approach is unique.” It is not. That is the entire somatic-therapy lineage.
- Safer: “Whelm is a written, self-led practice for working with overwhelm specifically, in the lineage of Focusing, RAIN, and FACE.”

## 5. Positioning options

### (a) Independent synthesis crediting lineage

Whelm is Lorin’s own intuitive synthesis. The case study acknowledges, on first mention, that the moves recombine elements from RAIN (Brach), FACE (Liggett), Focusing (Gendlin), Window of Tolerance (Siegel), and IFS (Schwartz). Lorin does not claim Whelm was derived from these — she found them after the fact — and the case study says so plainly.

*Tradeoffs:* Most honest framing. Reads as intellectually mature: a designer who made a thing intuitively and then situated it in the literature. Risk: hiring managers from clinical backgrounds may still want to know whether Lorin has trained in any of these modalities. Mitigate by being clear that Whelm is a *design product*, not a therapy.

### (b) Co-built with Catherine Liggett as a FACE-extended practice

Reach out to Liggett, frame Whelm as a written/sifting extension of FACE’s in-the-moment somatic protocol, and credit her as a collaborator or inspiration.

*Tradeoffs:* Gives Whelm immediate lineage credibility and removes the “is this a parallel invention?” question. But it requires Liggett’s actual buy-in, and it ties Whelm’s positioning to a single small-corpus practitioner. If Liggett is enthusiastic, this is the strongest option. If she is lukewarm, (a) is safer.

### (c) Whelm as a designer’s research artifact, not a method

Reframe entirely: the case study isn’t presenting Whelm as a regulation framework, it’s presenting it as *a designer’s attempt to externalize her own overwhelm into a tool, and what she learned about the existing landscape afterward.* The case study becomes about the design process — the noticing, the building, the landscape mapping — rather than about the framework as a clinical contribution.

*Tradeoffs:* This is the most defensible from a “don’t overclaim” perspective and shows off Lorin’s research instinct (she built first, situated second). Risk: a hiring manager skimming the homepage may miss the framework altogether and only see “a designer made a feelings app.” The case study has to earn the depth on the page.

**Recommendation:** Default to (a) unless Liggett is reachable and warm to (b). (c) is a strong third option if Lorin wants the case study to foreground process over product — which her overall portfolio voice already does.

## 7. Gaps and cautions

- **FACE has a tiny public corpus.** One blog post lays out the acronym; the rest is site copy and audio. If Whelm leans on FACE as primary lineage, Lorin should consider contacting Liggett directly for confirmation and attribution language. No public source located connects FACE to RAIN; do not claim that link unless Liggett confirms it.
- **No peer-reviewed evidence for RAIN as a discrete protocol.** Adjacent self-compassion research (MSC) supports the mechanism; the named four-step sequence has not, to this researcher’s ability to find, been independently tested. Cite RAIN as practitioner lineage, not as evidence.
- **Polyvagal Theory has been substantively critiqued in the scientific literature** on the comparative-anatomy claims that ground it. It is enormously influential clinically; it is *not* uncontested science. If Whelm uses polyvagal vocabulary, treat it as influential framework, not as settled neuroscience.
- **IFS, SE, and Hakomi are practitioner-developed modalities** with growing but uneven peer-reviewed bases. They are legitimate references for design work; they would be lighter ground for clinical claims.
- **Ideological loads to be aware of:**
  - *RAIN / MBSR / mindfulness lineage:* rooted in Theravada Buddhism (Vipassana). Generally framed as secular but carries a contemplative lineage that some readers will name and some won’t.
  - *IFS:* has expanded toward spiritual / Self-as-soul framings in recent work; some practitioners are uncomfortable with that drift, others embrace it.
  - *FACE / Liggett:* drawn from a shadow-work / inner-child / Jungian frame, which has overlap with psychodynamic and somatic lineages but also overlaps with the spiritually-coded coaching ecosystem. Lorin should be clear-eyed about which audience that signals to.
  - *Polyvagal Theory:* heavily marketed in the trauma-informed coaching space; carries a self-help connotation that can read as either accessible or pop-science depending on the audience.
- **None of these frameworks centers structural causes of overwhelm.** Same blind spot as the foundation doc — caregiving asymmetry, precarity, racialized labor, climate dread. If Whelm wants to interrogate why overwhelm is rising as a *cultural* condition and not just an individual experience, none of these references will carry that argument. That would need separate sources (e.g., Hartmut Rosa on social acceleration, Anne Helen Petersen on burnout, the Frontiers 2025 overwhelm study which gestures at workload pressures).
- **The “Portal” move is the most ideologically loaded.** Reframing overwhelm as *opportunity* is close to “your trauma is a teacher” language, which has real critical pushback from disability-justice and trauma-survivor communities. Whelm’s framing should acknowledge that not every overwhelmed moment is a portal, and that for some bodies in some conditions the priority is regulation and rest, not extraction of meaning. Be careful not to instrumentalize distress.
