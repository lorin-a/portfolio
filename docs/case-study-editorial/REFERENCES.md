## B. Portfolio feel (the designer-not-salesman register)

- **Michaël Bernard** ❤️ — see row above. The north star for *restraint + emotion*.
- **Florent Biffi** (florentbiffi.com) — whitespace-driven, type and space carry
  the story over decorative color. Substantial margins between sections invite a
  pause. Social proof (awards, logos) dense but **never aggressive**. "Easter
  eggs" / micro-interactions reward curiosity inside projects. Positions the
  *human* (graphic-design training, "sensitive to visual details") before the
  work. Microcopy with personality ("lose myself in the Internet").

## C. Layout / motion / pacing elegance

- **Studio Simms** (studio-simms.com) — *JS/Framer site; could not machine-fetch.*
  Lorin's read, verbatim: "white space can be your friend, grid is everything,
  scroll and animation create immersion, micro-interaction creates subtle
  engagement." The discipline reference: confidence to leave space.
- **Studio Simms / ByHeart** (studio-simms.com/work/byheart-brand-world-refresh)
  — **caution, not a model.** Lorin: "gorgeous but too image-heavy, more a
  graphic-design portfolio than a research one." Lesson: imagery must be
  *evidence of a claim*, not a gallery. Do not let visuals outrun the research.
- **Buck / 2x4 Foundation** (buck.co/work/2x4-foundation) — systematic
  alternation: **context → full-bleed visual proof**, one concept isolated per
  vertical section. Full-bleed images "aren't decorative; they demonstrate the
  principle discussed immediately beforehand." Generous serif headline scale.
  **No cramped galleries or carousels** — each image gets dedicated real estate.
  "The layout honors content rather than competing with it."
- **Hex Pens** (hexpens.com) — **single-element isolation + progressive
  disclosure.** Background graphics (atoms, DNA, hexagons) emerge as isolated
  layers one at a time. Product broken down feature-by-feature *sequentially*
  (ink capacity → nib → end cap), each with whitespace pause points. "Attention
  through progressive disclosure rather than simultaneous visual chaos."
- **Vogue — "Gen-Z broke the marketing funnel"** (vogue.com) — *blocked from
  machine-fetch.* Lorin's read: "too flashy but fascinating and great pace."
  The pacing reference for long-form: measured rhythm that pulls you through.

## D. What the whole corpus agrees on (the build brief)

Distilled across all of the above — especially the two LOVEs:

1. **Research as reasoning, not decoration.** Every image, stat, and quote is
   *evidence for a decision* (CNN, Buck, IKEA, ByHeart-as-caution). This is the
   single loudest, most repeated signal.
2. **One focal point at a time.** Isolate and disclose progressively; never
   simultaneous visual chaos (Hex Pens, Buck, Michaël Bernard). Cures "my eyes
   bounce."
3. **Problem-first hook with stakes that stay visible.** A provocative
   opener, quantified, carried through (CNN). Lorin's question-hero already does
   this.
4. **Questions as section openers.** Propulsive, conversational, walks the
   reader through the reasoning (Komoot, Beakery).
5. **First-person ownership + concrete specifics.** "I led / I wrote / I
   secured," real numbers and named people (Beakery, Explora, IKEA). Groundswell's
   biggest gap (still institutional "we").
6. **Restraint and breath.** White space is structural; each beat breathes; type
   and space carry it over decorative color (Michaël Bernard, Florent Biffi,
   Studio Simms, Buck).
7. **A few aphoristic, earned lines.** Short italic principles that distill
   process into wisdom — used sparingly (CNN's three principles).
8. **Imagery is captioned proof, full-bleed, never a carousel** (Buck, ByHeart
   caution).

**The form these imply** (Groundswell instance): a single centered reading column
that falls like a long-form feature, punctuated by full-bleed cinematic
act-dividers — dark claim → light reading + inline evidence → full-bleed photo
proof → dark claim. Sense / Weave / Shape as the three breaths, bookended by the
question-hero and a quiet close. Palette = the real homepage tokens
(sage #A8B5A0 / deep #5E6B54, cream #FBF9F6, ink #2C2C28), so it belongs.
