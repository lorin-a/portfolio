# Client Project: Birthstory, a Micro-App for Birth Narratives

**About: **Design a micro-app to accompany Myana, a pregnancy app, that helps birthing parents log important medical information, record details about their birth experience, and reflect on the experience afterward. **Client: **University of Pittsburgh Women’s Health Researchers

**App Requirements:**

**Information gathering: **Medical info, Contextual info, Birth narrative, Feelings

**Meaning making: **Processing App, Administration Profile/Onboarding, Nudges

**Duration: **6 weeks

We wanted our app to be functional, intuitive, soothing, and efficient.

[IMAGE 3] caption: (none)

We focused our early iterations on 4 key user flows.

[IMAGE 4] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 5] caption: (none)

And initially created a simple flow that separated the app’s functions into two main categories: Document and Reflect.

[IMAGE 6] caption: (none)

We wanted the interface to feel calming, knowing that this app would be used in wee hours of the morning or in harsh hospital light. We intended for it to feel organic and emotionally supportive—creating safe space for difficult emotions and uplifting sentiments. We also wanted it to feel similar to the parent app, Myana, so we created a new gradient to match Myana’s gradient style.

[IMAGE 7] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 8] caption: (none)

We explored lo-fi ideas for how the app could feel and what features it could support. We found that we were struggling to narrow the focus of the app such that functioned as a micro-app and did not compete with Myana’s pregnancy features. We received feedback in class critiques that informed our V2 wireframes which we took to user research testing.

## Final Iteration

[IMAGE 23] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 24] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 25] caption: (none)

Users open the app and are quickly onboarded to the app’s functions with examples of how to use it.

[IMAGE 26] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 27] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 28] caption: (none)

Within the app, users can easily toggle between notes and journal—language that indicates use-case intentions of document + reflect. Entries are logged chronologically in a vertical timeline and all posts are tagged with keywords for easy search later.

[IMAGE 29] caption: (none)

Users shuffle through thoughtful prompts that allow them to reflect on their birth experience with nudges to review them later.

[IMAGE 30] caption: (none)

We heard many times in our interviews that users valued hearing about the experience of their loved ones as part of the birth story and would like ways to collaborate with them in an app like this. So we added a feature called the Care Pod where users can curate an inner circle that is invited to the app to share supportive messages and receive updates on how the birth is going.

[IMAGE 31] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 32] caption: (none)

Lastly, all users said they don’t fully trust apps to preserve their story and crave a physical take away that they can keep. We added a Birth Story Book feature where journal entries and notes are selected to be added to the print version of the birth story—accessible via free PDF or affordable booklet ordered within the app.

> It would be tragic to loose those moments if the app disappeared someday.
