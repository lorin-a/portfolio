# **Communications System: Rebranding the Great Allegheny Passage Trail**

## Visual Communications Studio: Brand Project

**Assignment: **Choose a local business or organization that performs a service you think is necessary but can benefit from (better)* visual communication design. Using design research methods, identify issues and problems within your chosen business or organization. Identify various “touch points” for the user throughout their experience and how these could be improved with visual communication design. Your solution must include at least four deliverables.

**Duration:** 4 Weeks

**Team: **Lorin Anderberg, Michael Juan, Rui Ying

**Class:** Visual Communications Studio I, Brett Yasko

[IMAGE 2] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 3] caption: (none)

**Approach: **My teammates and I all love nature and the outdoors, so we set out to find a local organization that combined our passion for nature and that had strong potential for visual communications improvement. We landed on the Great Allegheny Passage, a 150-mile non-motorized path that goes from the tip of Pittsburgh to Cumberland, MD. The existing website and brand are good so we decided to narrow our focus to bike touring the trail as a beginner.

**Reflection: **We were able to speak with the GAP association and learned that previous CMU grads designed the existing website and that they explored using an app in the past. We felt an enormous amount of potential from this project for real world results.

[IMAGE 9] caption: (none)

**Field Research: **We drove out to Ohiopyle, the trail’s most popular trail town, to observe signage and speak to folks on the trail about bike touring. We also spoke to 30 friends, family members, and CMU students about the GAP trail and bike touring. Of those interviews, only one person said they were not interested in bike touring at all. This confirmed the potential that these solutions would be useful to our target users.

**User Research: **We developed personas and mapped out their journeys while planning a bike tour to identify key pain points and opportunities for our deliverables to be positive interventions. We translated the most important pain points into key deliverables that would serve our user’s needs at every stage of their journey.

**Reflection: **Our research led us to the conclusion that our four main deliverables would be:

- Apparel (awareness + nostalgia)

- Signage + trail mural (awareness + wayfinding)

- Gap Guide Pamphlet (awareness + education + initiation)

- Gap Map App (trip planner, tool, education, community)

**Iterations and Feedback: **We received productive feedback at our weekly check-ins and presentations that helped us alter each deliverable to its most optimal state. The main feedback was to get our style aligned and go for a line-art approach and to make sure everything felt cohesive. We decided to incorporate apparel in case our signage or mural idea didn’t pan out. We spent the majority of our time on the most time-consuming deliverables with the plan to adjust our expectations as the deadline approached.

[IMAGE 43] caption: (none)

**Challenges: **The Gap Guide would serve as an informative overview of the trail, inviting beginners to explore the Gap Map App and learn about bike touring. Features:

- Recommended itineraries

- Info about the App

- An overview of trail attractions

- A visual packing list

- Maybe a set of stickers to collect

At first, we struggled to come up with a consistent illustration style since each of us had a different style and skillset. We did not have a clear vision for the signage or road mural but felt that once we finished the app, pamphlet, and brand it would make sense based on how it all fit together cohesively. We eventually decided to drop working on the website’s trip planner and focus solely on the App which was the right choice in the end because our deliverables were robust enough as it is.
