# SomeBuddy: A Social App for Busy Grad Students

**About:** Final project for Carnegie Mellon School of Design MA Interaction Design Studio II, Spring 2025

**Ask:** Develop a design ecosystem with at least one off-screen element that solves a practical problem. The project must include primary research and user feedback.

**Team: **Lorin Anderberg, Yaezi Lee, Rui Ying

**Class: **Interaction Design Studio II, Ashley Deal + Raelynn O’Leary

[IMAGE 2] caption: (none)

[EMBED: YouTube video "SomeBuddy: A Social App for Grad Students" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdlaitoGycA]

As CMU grad students, we’ve all felt isolated at times. The workload never stops, and it’s hard to find time to hang out with friends, let alone meet new people outside of class.

We kept hearing this from others, too. People want to connect, but it’s hard to make it happen.

So we asked ourselves:

**How can we create easy, low-pressure ways for grad students to connect through simple activities , especially when it’s something they don’t want to do alone?**

That question guided our research. Let’s explore how we arrived at SomeBuddy.

**We focused our research on four key objectives:**

**First**, we wanted to **identify the types of activities** students don’t want to — or simply can’t — do alone. This helped us see what kinds of moments are most likely to benefit from social support.

**Second**, we looked at the **barriers** that make it hard for students to find others to do these activities with — things like time, coordination, or social hesitation.

**Third**, we explored what makes students feel **comfortable connecting with new people**, especially in casual, low-stakes situations.

**4th** Understand the **qualities students look for in a companion** for different kinds of activities — whether that’s shared goals or personality fit

These objectives shaped how we planned our user research and what questions we asked.

[IMAGE 6] caption: (none)

We made an ecosystem map to lay out all of the aspects of social isolation at CMU touching touchpoints and components and understand what drives social isolation at CMU

We looked at things like what makes it hard to meet new people, find shared interests, sync schedules, and feel a sense of belonging.

This helped us see the bigger picture — and also made us aware of our own assumptions, so we could test them through user research

[IMAGE 7] caption: (none)

**From the 8 interviews we did with grad students, we learned a few important things:**

**First,** the kind of connection students want really depends on the activity. For quick, everyday tasks — like studying or running errands — they’re fine pairing up with almost anyone. But for things that feel more personal or involve safety, like hiking, they’d rather know a bit more about the other person first.

**Second,** students are drawn to short, casual, and last-minute meetups. Their schedules are packed, so the easier it is to join something without planning too far ahead

[IMAGE 8] caption: (none)

[IMAGE 9] caption: (none)

**Our interviews gave us direction on our design principles. We understood that our solution should:**

- **Support Spontaneity:** by making last minute plans with minimal friction.

- **Low-Effort Coordination:** it should make coordinating plans simple and easy

- **Normalize Low-Pressure Interactions: **the nature of the design should encourage casual hangs, drop-ins, and opt-out-friendly experiences to reduce the burden in commitment.

- **Accommodate Exhaustion: **for our users who are busy, let’s make this experience more time efficient

- **Avoid Awkwardness: **by setting expectations beforehand.

[IMAGE 10] caption: (none)

We combined what we heard from our user interviews into three main user categories that we personified with user personas. First is the Logistical Task Mate who prefers spontaneous opportunities to achieve her goals with others.

[IMAGE 11] caption: (none)

Next, is the casual connector who values companionship for activities and intellectual discussions, seeking, low-effort social experiences amid a busy schedule.

[IMAGE 12] caption: (none)

And finally, the friendship seeker who is looking for more depth, values both academic discussions and activity-based meetups but struggles to find time in her busy schedule.

[IMAGE 28] caption: (none)

Within the SomeBuddy app, finding someone to do something with is super quick and low-pressure.

Instead of filling out a bunch of forms, users just go through a simple setup — pick an activity or task they want to do, choose a general time and location, and then post.

Our goal is to make the experience fast, effortless, and low-pressure.

Once its posted, the app helps surface nearby matches. Users can filter potential buddies by **distance, age, gender, and shared interests** using both keyword search and filter tools. . Results appear in both **map** and **list views**.

From there, there’s another layer of filters depending on the activity — so if you’re looking for someone who’s craving for espresso or having a casual chat while grabbing coffee, you can narrow it down even more.

[IMAGE 29] caption: (none)

When you find someone you want to reach out to, you’ve got two options:

- You can either **send a request** using a pre-filled message template that only requires a quick time adjustment.

- Or, if you’d rather **chat first**, you can open a message with pre-set conversation starters. We’ve built in templates to make sure it doesn’t feel like too much work or pressure. You can easily respond using **simple accept or decline buttons**, which avoids the need for long replies.

[IMAGE 30] caption: (none)

Badge customization allows users to customize unique color, text, shape, and type color according to their preferences for each badge rewarded to them for completing a task together with another Somebuddy or in app achievements.

[IMAGE 31] caption: (none)

Users have access to the “My badge collection” page where they can view all their collected or rewarded badges.

Then is the bingo card feature. It started as part of our **Orientation Bingo Card**, and users can keep the fun going by joining new bingo cards organized by different themes and categories — like outdoor, thrifting, food or sth like that and they can **create their own custom bingo card**, set the own challenges, and share it with others.

It’s a light, playful way to turn everyday activities into social moments — and discover more SomeBuddies along the way.
