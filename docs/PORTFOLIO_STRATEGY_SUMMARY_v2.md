# Lorin.work Portfolio — Strategy & Creative Direction Summary
## Session: February 20, 2026 (Complete)

---

## 1. THE THESIS

**Structure and heart coexist.** Not as opposites to balance, but as a unified way of seeing. Lorin brings rigor to emotional spaces and humanity to systematic ones. The site should *perform* this — not state it. The grid itself should feel warm. The data should feel handmade. The methodology should feel like compassion. Visitors should not be able to tell where the structure ends and the heart begins.

This thesis is embodied in Groundswell (rigorous research AS an act of care for burned-out healthcare workers) and in Lorin's identity: both fire and water, both engineer and poet.

**Design implication:** No split-screen metaphors (visually argues the two sides are separate). Instead, design moments where structure IS the expression — where the careful grid IS the warmth, where the precise typography IS the emotion.

## 2. THE EMOTIONAL TARGET

### First 3 Seconds
The hiring manager should feel: **inspired, refreshed, and think "finally."** Someone who balances creativity, story, and interaction to be authentic without overdoing it with gimmicks. Personality with class. Unique brilliance that would be an add to their team.

### The North Star
> "One of the most intentional portfolios I've seen. Clear attention to detail, care in every component, this person cares deeply and has range."

### Warmth Strategy
The "I want to be her friend" quality is **discovered, not performed.** It lands when someone notices how she credited collaborators, reads how she describes her participants, or catches a tiny playful detail they almost missed. Earned warmth > declared warmth.

## 3. THE CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK: Four Elemental Energies

The classical elements (Water, Fire, Air, Earth) serve as an **invisible design system** — guiding color, texture, motion, and structure behind the scenes. They are NOT labeled on the site. The visitor feels them without naming them. The attentive visitor who lingers may discover the pattern.

The door stays open for the elements to surface through icons, custom section titles, or hidden details — decided section by section as the design evolves.

### Water — The Holder
The nurturer. The safe place to land. The walking "home." Creates conditions where truth can surface. In work: trauma-informed facilitation, participatory research, care for participant voices.

### Fire — The Advocate
The passionate protector. The driver of the ship. Takes what she heard in the safe room and pushes it upstream to people with power. In work: institutional recommendations, grant proposals, refusing to let community wisdom get filed away.

### Air — The Translator
The reader of hidden language. The clairvoyant. Sees what isn't being said and names it. In work: pattern recognition, systems thinking, making the invisible visible.

### Earth — The Builder
The bringer of ideas into being. The organizer. The fixer. Takes insight (Air), passion (Fire), and trust (Water) and turns it into something real. In work: toolkits, brand systems, campaigns, prototypes.

### Mapping to Existing Pillars
| Pillar | Element | Tension Pairing |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| Systems | Air | Meticulous Dreamer |
| Stories | Water + Fire | Playful Perfectionist |
| Solutions | Earth | Hopeful Realist |

## 5. VISUAL LANGUAGE

### Confirmed Direction
- **Polished foundation with experimental moments** — precise grid, but what lives inside it breathes and surprises
- **Typography-forward** — type IS the design (inspired by Olha, Flow, Off Season, Self Aware)
- **Organic textures, natural materials** — stone, paper grain, earth tones, things that feel printed/pressed/held
- **Patina quality** — nothing glossy or new; textured like something with history
- **Restrained palette** — richness from texture and scale contrast, not color variety
- **Calm, smooth animations** — organic easing, contributing to narrative, never jarring or hyperactive

### Color Palette: Derived from Lorin's Scanned Objects
The palette already exists in the things she loves:
- **Sage green** — aventurine stone, protea leaves, green jasper
- **Plum/purple** — childhood finger paintings, amethyst
- **Terracotta/amber** — childhood paintings, altar cloth stains, warm earth tones
- **Chalcedony blue** — blue stones, apatite
- **Cream/gold** — altar cloth linen, paper backgrounds

This maps to the existing palette (olive, terracotta, plum, sage, cream). No need to start fresh — refine what's there and root it in the scans.

### The Scanner Scans: Core Visual Asset
Lorin's flatbed scanner scans of personal objects (stones, dried flowers, altar cloth, childhood paintings, earrings, embroidered textile, ceramic knobs, vintage pottery) are extraordinary and serve as **raw material for the entire visual system.**

#### How they can be used (spectrum from subtle to central):
- **Texture extraction:** Linen grain, stone surfaces, embroidery threads as background textures, hover states, section dividers
- **CSS mix-blend-mode:** Scanned textures interacting with colored backgrounds
- **CSS mask-image:** Text or shapes revealing scanned texture underneath (e.g., name in large type filled with embroidered floral textile)
- **Cutouts with transparent backgrounds:** Individual stones, leaves, flowers floating on cream backgrounds
- **Parallax objects:** Cutout objects drifting at different speeds as you scroll
- **Dark-field section breaks:** Full-bleed scanned compositions as moments of quiet beauty between case studies
- **Generative texture:** Sampling colors from childhood paintings programmatically for gradients or effects
- **Timeline landmarks:** Specific objects appearing along the journey path
- **About section collection:** One place where the full collection appears and the visitor understands this person's aesthetic is how they actually see the world

#### The dark-field scanner aesthetic
The black background gives every object its own gravity. Nothing competes. Each thing gets to just BE. Feels like Dutch still-life paintings meets natural history specimen plates — intimate, pressed-close, like looking into a cabinet of curiosities.

## 6. INTERACTION PHILOSOPHY

### Core Principle
**Create conditions for discovery. Don't perform.**

Mirrors Lorin's participatory design practice: she builds the container, the participant (visitor) makes the discovery. Interaction should feel like a small ritual or invitation, not a spectacle.

### Confirmed Interaction Ideas
- Scroll-triggered reveals and animations throughout (GSAP ScrollTrigger)
- Timeline page as primary interactive experience
- Tarot card reveal mechanic — curated cards face-down, click to discover facets of who Lorin is. Not random — curated. The act of choosing to reveal mirrors participatory design philosophy.
- Scanned objects as interactive elements (hover, click, discover)
- Click-to-activate rather than auto-playing (respect both scanners and explorers)

### Under Consideration
- Role/identity that shifts contextually (like Olha's nav label)
- Subtle texture or element responding to cursor
- "Choose your path" navigation moments
- Site color palette preference selector

### Animation Guidelines
- All motion: smooth, organic, natural, calm-paced. Never jarring.
- Easing curves defined early as part of design system emotional language
- Scroll-triggered viewport detection for enter-animations
- CSS sticky positioning for scroll-based reveals
- Text splitting for typographic animation (with accessibility safeguards)
- Staggered reveals for groups of elements (stones appearing one by one, cards revealing in sequence)

### Anti-Patterns
- Nothing hyperactive or narrative-losing
- No gimmicks — every interaction must do conceptual or storytelling work
- No split-screen as metaphor for the thesis
- No auto-playing anything that might overwhelm

## 7. CONTENT & COPY DIRECTION

### Voice
Specific, warm, a little poetic, grounded in real details. Written the way Lorin actually talks. The about-me draft is the benchmark:

> "My dad is an engineer who takes things apart to understand how they work. My mom is a special education teacher who pays attention to the words people use and what they reveal. Between them, I learned that everything worth caring about has both a structure and a story — and I've been following that thread ever since, through journalism, marketing, caregiving, through countries I'd never been to by foot and bicycle, always listening for what wasn't being said and wondering why nothing had been built to hold it. Design is where that thread finally wove together, and now I want to keep pulling it — into healthcare, education, and the quiet, stubborn systems that shape how people care for each other."

### Content Hierarchy for Hiring Managers
1. What you do and what domain (immediately clear)
2. Evidence of impact (case study outcomes visible fast)
3. How you think (process revealed through your decisions)
4. Who you are (discovered through voice, details, care — not declared)

### What Needs Rework
- **Tagline:** "I translate community wisdom into systems change through stories" — accurate but invisible. Replace with something only Lorin could write.
- **Pillar text:** Explanatory text goes flat. Tension pairings (meticulous dreamer, etc.) do the heavy lifting. Consider leading with contradictions, explaining second.
- **Three-pillar structure:** Open to rethinking entirely around elemental framework or tension pairings.

### Poster Work: Special Treatment
Lorin's poster work demonstrating "huge concept to refined simple metaphor" deserves its own mini case study or process reveal — NOT a gallery item. This is evidence of the single most valuable design skill: taking complexity and making it land instantly. Could be titled something like "From Concept to Clarity."

### Voice-Over Animation: Strategic Placement
The animation where you hear Lorin's voice is one of the strongest assets — the only medium where people can feel her actual presence. Should be embedded somewhere prominent (timeline transition point or about section), not buried.

## 8. TECHNICAL STACK & TOOLS

### Confirmed
- **Framework:** Next.js with CSS Modules
- **Animation Library:** GSAP (now fully free) with ScrollTrigger plugin
  - Core methods: `.to`, `.from`, `.fromTo`, timelines
  - ScrollTrigger for scroll-based animations
  - SplitText for typographic animation
  - Stagger for sequential reveals
- **Media Hosting:** Cloudinary for optimized images/video
- **Deployment:** Vercel
- **Domain:** lorin.work
- **Version Control:** GitHub

### Animation Techniques We'll Use (from PDFs)
1. **Scroll tracking** — GSAP ScrollTrigger, returning 0-1 progress values for animation
2. **Viewport detection** — animate elements on enter (IntersectionObserver via GSAP)
3. **CSS sticky positioning** — for scroll-based reveals and pinned sections
4. **Intentional easing curves** — defined early, sets the emotional mood of the site
5. **Text splitting** — individual letter/word animation for key typographic moments
6. **Stagger animations** — sequential reveals with slight delays
7. **Smooth scrolling** — Lenis or similar for fluid scroll experience
8. **Map function** — transforming scroll progress into animation values (scale, opacity, position)

### NOT Using
- Three.js / WebGL (too heavy, wrong aesthetic, maintenance burden)
- Tailwind CSS (using CSS Modules for more control)
- Heavy 3D models

### Keeping in Reserve
- Subtle CSS 3D transforms (perspective, rotateX/Y) for tactile hover effects
- Canvas API for potential generative texture from childhood painting colors
- mix-blend-mode and mask-image for scan texture integration

## 9. DESIGN PRINCIPLES (Consolidated from All PDFs)

### From Web Design Best Practices
1. **Easing sets the mood.** Define easing curves early as part of design system.
2. **60/30/10 color rule.** 60% dominant/neutral, 30% brand, 10% accent.
3. **Accessibility is non-negotiable.** WCAG AA minimum. Contrast checkers always.
4. **Craft + Soul > Process.** Portfolio demonstrates making people feel cared for, not just that a process was followed.
5. **Creative direction starts with words.** Define goals and voice before touching code.
6. **Diagnose before prescribing.** Understand the problem before designing the solution.
7. **Features don't sell, stories do.** Case studies lead with transformation, not methodology.
8. **Visual hierarchy over layout patterns.** Most important = biggest/boldest, then turn down volume.
9. **No ghost buttons.** High-contrast CTAs.
10. **Design for the audience** (hiring managers), not yourself.

### From Advanced Web Design
11. **GSAP is the industry standard** for scroll-triggered web animation.
12. **ScrollTrigger** powers pinned sections, progress-based animation, and scroll-driven reveals.
13. **Timeline sequencing** allows choreographed multi-element animations.
14. **Performance matters** — optimize for mobile, lazy-load heavy assets.

### File Structure & Professionalism
15. **The stranger test:** Could someone who's never seen the project jump in and understand everything?
16. **Every visual property appearing more than once = a variable.**
17. **Clean, documented, hand-off-ready code.**
